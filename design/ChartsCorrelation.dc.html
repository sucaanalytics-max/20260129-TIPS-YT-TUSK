Across three metrics × two companies × fifteen lags, fewer results reach nominal significance than pure noise would produce — and none survive Benjamini–Hochberg correction within their family (best p = 0.0246 against a 0.0033 threshold). All three nominal hits are TIPSMUSIC releases, and they include a sign flip — +0.256 at lag −7, +0.235 at −6, then −0.245 at −3 — which is what noise looks like, not a lead–lag mechanism. Adjacent lags are autocorrelated, so they are not independent evidence either.

Correlations use daily log returns, not the price level: two trending series will correlate with almost anything, and that artefact is the most common way this analysis is got wrong. On this evidence, none of views, subscriber growth or release cadence has a demonstrable relationship with the share price at any lag inside a week.
